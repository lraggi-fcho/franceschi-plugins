# Stage 2b — Verify on the company's own website

Free, parallelisable across subagents, and the highest-yield stage in the pipeline. **Five jobs in one pass** — the site is already open.

### 1. The verdict

Find an explicit statement, in the company's own words, that they are what the profile says a prospect is. **Quote it verbatim with its URL.** No quote, no top tier. A site that is down, parked or robots-blocked is "REVIEW — unconfirmed" and mid-tier, never a guess.

**Vendor keyword tags are self-descriptions and they are wrong in both directions.** Measured: two companies carrying the right tags were the wrong business model entirely; three genuine prospects carried none of the tags and surfaced only when the keyword gate was dropped. Tag-only qualification would have put two anti-prospects at the top of the call list.

**Never infer the model from a company name or domain.** A domain that spells out the service still needs on-page proof.

**A 404 on the obvious page is evidence, and it cuts both ways.** For one company, four candidate pages 404ing plus a three-URL sitemap confirmed the anti-prospect. For three others the offer was real but buried on a plant page, at the foot of a packaging page, or as two phrases inside an About narrative — which is the pitch, not a disqualification. Read the whole site before deciding which case you have.

**Budget for the cull.** The reference pipeline stops 25% of candidates here. Over-select by a third: to contact 8, select 12. Verification is free; picking too few candidates is the real constraint.

### 2. Harvest the contacts the site gives away — before spending anything

Companies publish more than the vendor holds. Measured: one company's contact page listed four named executives with direct addresses, cost zero. Another's product catalog PDF named a **Director of Sales who does not exist in the vendor's 23-person roster at all** — so enrichment routed to a CEO on a catchall parent domain for a credit, and the catalog had the better contact for free.

Check the contact page, the about/team page, any downloadable catalog or capabilities PDF, and the footer. Enrich only what the site withholds.

**Where the site and the vendor disagree about a person, the site wins** — and record the disagreement rather than quietly resolving it. Vendor records go stale: one named the wrong CEO, another had dropped the ledger's contact entirely.

### 3. Reconcile the engagement shape

**This step decides the shape the score only guessed at.** Anything that shows a commercial function already exists flips it:

- a named sales or commercial leader anywhere on the site
- sales history in the enriched contact's own employment record
- an address the company itself calls a *commercial* headquarters rather than a plant
- a parent company

On the reference pipeline three leads were queued to be told "nobody is selling your capacity" — one addressed to a Chief Commercial Officer, one to the VP of Sales, one to a career sales executive. All three were caught by hand, none by the protocol.

**Telling someone their function does not exist, when their job is that function, is the single most damaging error this pipeline can make.** It is not a near miss. The reader knows their own organisation better than we do, and the email is unrecoverable.

### 4. Check ownership

A thin subsidiary looks exactly like a company with no commercial function. Measured: a company scored near the top of the list and had been owned by a large parent for five years — the signal was an artifact of the subsidiary's thin public footprint.

But **verify the parent rather than assuming it.** A different apparent parent turned out to have no website at all: a wildcard redirect onto the subsidiary, `/about` and `/brands` 404, its name appearing nowhere on the subsidiary's site. A parent that publishes nothing has no commercial function to redirect to — the subsidiary is the buyer.

Signals: an enriched contact whose email is on a different domain from the company, an employment record naming two organisations, an unfamiliar entity in the footer or copyright line.

### 5. Check the domain belongs to the company

Vendors attach the wrong site. Measured: a company listed in one state whose domain served a different company two thousand miles away. It scored well enough for the top tier on numbers alone. Hold as "REVIEW — identity unconfirmed" and resolve before writing anything, because describing the wrong company back to a prospect is unrecoverable.

### Unreachable leads

A lead with no reachable channel does not get silently skipped — that is how the highest-scoring company in a book goes untouched for nine days. Source a channel by hand (contact page, catalog PDF, press release, trade directory), or mark "REVIEW — no channel" and hold it visibly.
