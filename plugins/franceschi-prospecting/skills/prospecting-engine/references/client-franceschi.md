## 2. The thesis (ICP, as defined by Luisina, 2026-10-16)

**Approach principle (Luisina, 2026-10-16):** first learn what the prospect offers and what it needs. Then match that to our lines, using the catalogue as the map. Do not limit the search to what we assume they buy, because they may surprise us. Products are the match at the end, not the starting filter.

- **Prospect:** U.S.-based CPG food brands and food manufacturers making premium snacks, natural and better-for-you foods, and gourmet treats. Priority goes to companies that **manufacture their own products** and could use chocolate chips, chunks, wafers, compound chocolate or cocoa powder as ingredients. Product families: chocolate-containing products, baked goods and cookies, bars, confectionery, snacks.
- **Seed look-alikes** (use for Apollo similar-company search): Superior Snacks, Too Good Gourmet, Avatar Natural Foods.
- **Priority order:** manufacturers first, then brands that manufacture in-house, then resellers (lowest).
- **Anti-prospect (looks identical in the data):** retailers; distributors; brands that only resell finished products; cacao importers, brokers and traders; other cacao or chocolate suppliers (competitors); cafés and chocolate shops that do not manufacture.
- **Co-packed brands count (decided by Luisina, 2026-10-16):** a brand that outsources manufacturing to a co-packer is a prospect. Score it on the product it sells, not on whether it owns a plant. Verification must still confirm that the brand's own product contains or could contain chocolate or cocoa.
- **Target decision-makers:** Procurement and Sourcing Managers, R&D and Product Development Managers, Operations Managers, and Founders and CEOs of small and mid-sized manufacturers.
- **Value drivers the buyer cares about:** high-quality cacao, distinctive flavor, consistent supply, product differentiation.
- **Why the prospect has the problem (hypothesis, to validate):** a brand whose product is built on flavor needs a chocolate supplier that can match a spec and keep it consistent. **Validate on the first batch:** does the buyer's own site talk about its ingredients or flavor, and does that lead to a purchase conversation?

> **Settle before the first run:** the product families have different angles and sizes. The first batch should run on one family, not all of them.

## 3. Entry conditions — pass/fail, zero points

- **Business model:** manufactures its own food product (snacks, baked goods, bars, confectionery, natural foods) that could contain chocolate or cocoa. Retailer, distributor, or reseller of finished product only → Tier C.
- **Size:** 1–250 employees. 251–500 caps at Tier B with "REVIEW — size band, confirm production scale".
- **Geography:** United States headquarters, US production or US commercial base.
- **Other hard gates:** not a cacao or chocolate supplier, importer or broker (competitor). Not a trade association or trade media. Not THE COFFEE CORP or any related entity (section 9).
- **Direct-to-consumer only = excluded (Luisina, 2026-10-16):** a business whose only sales channel is selling to the final consumer (B2C or B2P) is not a prospect. A manufacturer that *also* sells direct online stays in, because it buys ingredients and manufactures its own product. Verification records the sales channel: manufacturer with consumer sales → keep; consumer-only seller → exclude.
